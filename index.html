Build a factory that runs, then build it deeper. Working machines add up to active engineering volume, and volume buys precision: higher layers, finer models, deeper research. At the bottom of the world is the material for a transmitter. Something out there answers, but only with the mechanisms it has heard you use.

Getting started

You land with 8 iron plates, a burner drill and a stone furnace. Mine coal and stone by hand, put the drill on iron ore facing the furnace, and fuel both. Craft from there (E). Electricity comes from steam: a water pump on water, boilers burning fuel beside it, steam engines beside those, and small poles; machines only run inside a pole's blue supply area. Belts have two lanes (bus belts three); click any belt for its line and its network (also under L, Belt lines).

Reading the arrows

Hover or hold a building to see where things go. Blue: where an inserter picks up, or where one can feed a machine. Orange: where things come out. Green: something is feeding it. Red ✕: that won't work. Belts never load furnaces or assemblers directly: put an inserter between them. Drills push straight into whatever is in front. Alt hides the small arrows.

Logistics

Belts have two lanes, four items per lane per tile. A belt fed into the side of another puts its items on the lane on that side; a single side feed turns it into a curve. Inserters put items on the far lane, drills on the near one. Bus belts carry three lanes, take items only from behind, and any tile can send its left or right lane off to the side with a filter. Drills, furnaces, assemblers, labs and pumpjacks take augments (speed, efficiency, productivity; depth for drills). Deposits never run out: they go deeper (surface, depth, bedrock), and a drill needs to reach the layer. Inserters only take what the machine in front of them needs. Drills push straight into whatever is in front of them, or drop ore on the ground. Electric machines need a pole nearby; drag a belt over another to change it.

Power

Put a water pump on water, boilers against it, and steam engines against the boilers: one pump feeds 20 boilers, one boiler feeds 2 engines. Water and steam pass between buildings that touch. Engines only work as hard as the network needs; when it needs more than they give, every machine slows down. Poles of one voltage wire up by themselves. Hold a power cable and click two poles to link them from further away. High voltage power lines cover a 21×21 area; step down to low voltage with a transformer station and a transformer cable. A flashing plug means a machine has no power (red) or too little (yellow).

Oil and fluids

Pipes join every pipe and fluid port next to them; one line carries one fluid, and long lines carry less per second. Pumps push a line one way and restore full flow; valves pass one way only (or only overflow, or only to top up). Underground pipes jump up to 10 tiles under belts, roads and rails. Pumpjacks on crude oil feed refineries, which make heavy fuel oil, diesel, gasoline and later jet fuel. Barreling stations fill and empty barrels, so fluids can travel on belts and in vehicles.

Vehicles, roads, rails and ships

A vehicle factory joins an engine to a frame: petrol for cars, diesel for trucks, trains and tanks, heavy fuel oil for ships, jet fuel for the fastest. B gets in or out; W/S drive, A/D steer (or pick the branch on rails). Trucks only drive on roads, boats on water, trains on rails. Train stations, truck depots and ports store goods and fuel; vehicles refuel there and load or unload by their route. L opens routes: stops, cargo, when to leave, and loops. Roads are laid by dragging; the bar above your hand sets width, lanes, markings and sidewalks. Walking on paths and sidewalks is faster.

Factories

Research Factory planning to found more factories (K). Each is a separate network: belts, inserters, pipes and power never cross between them, so goods move from one to another only by truck, train or ship. J switches which factory you build for.

Fish

Hold right-click on a fish to catch it; hold it and click to eat. Boats with a fishing net catch what they pass, past a stack, but the net tears at two. Fished-out water takes a long time to recover.
